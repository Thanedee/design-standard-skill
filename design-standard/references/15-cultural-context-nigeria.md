# 15. Cultural context and localisation

Design for the actual audience: local people, places, language, terms, calendars and symbols. In Nigeria that means correct professional terminology, the naira sign and local number conventions, respect for religious and ethnic diversity, and no national emblems in commercial work without authority.

### 15.1 Universal localisation rules

- **Reading direction:** left-to-right languages read F and Z patterns; right-to-left languages (Arabic, Ajami Hausa script) mirror the layout, including icons that imply direction.
- **Text expansion:** translations can run 20–35% longer than English; leave space in layouts.
- **Formats:** dates, times, currency, phone numbers and addresses follow local convention.
- **Imagery:** local people, dress, architecture, landscapes, light and climate.
- **Gestures and symbols** differ by culture; check hand gestures, animals, numbers and colours before using them.

### 15.2 Designing for Nigerian audiences

| Area | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Currency | ₦ before the number (₦40,000; ₦40k; ₦40 m); check the font has the ₦ glyph |
| Professional terms | Use local practice: tenancy agreement (not lease) for most residential lets; Governor’s Consent; C of O; survey plan; caution fee; agency and legal fees |
| Titles | Use titles people use: ESV, Arc., Engr., Barr., Dr, Chief, Alhaji/Alhaja, Pastor, HRM; get them exactly right |
| Language | English for professional work; Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa for warmth and reach where the brand voice allows; native speakers check every word and tone mark |
| Places | Name real neighbourhoods (Lekki, Ikoyi, Yaba, Wuse, GRA) to signal relevance; show recognisable local architecture |
| Calendar | Plan around Christmas, Easter, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha (Sallah), Ramadan, Independence Day (1 Oct), Democracy Day (12 June), wedding seasons and school terms |
| Religion | Balance Christian and Muslim representation in general-audience work; avoid religious symbols in purely commercial contexts unless the client is faith-based |
| Ethnicity | Avoid ethnic stereotypes and jokes; represent the diversity of the audience |
| Politics | Avoid party colours, slogans and figures in commercial work, especially in election periods |
| Connectivity | Many viewers use mid-range Android phones on mobile data: light files, high contrast, legible on small screens (section 10.7) |

### 15.3 Colour and symbols

- See section 5.5: white (purity, holiness, some funerals), red (power, danger, passion), black (mourning), green and white (national identity).
- **National emblems (MUST NOT):** under section 6 of the Flags and Coats of Arms Act, using the National Flag or Coat of Arms, or close imitations, in connection with any business, trade or profession without authority is an offence ([Laws of Nigeria](https://www.placng.org/lawsofnigeria/print.php?sn=188)). **\[Law\]** This applies to AI images too: a generated “official” crest on a land title or certificate must be removed.
- Do not reproduce government, NIESV, ESVARBON, bank or other institutional seals and logos unless the client is authorised to use them and supplies the files.

### 15.4 Representation in Nigerian contexts

- Show Nigerian professionals as competent experts in modern settings, not only in traditional or rural scenes.
- Reflect the real mix of ages, genders and regions of the audience.
- Traditional attire (aso-oke, agbada, isiagu, babban riga, gele) should be accurate to the culture shown and appropriate to the occasion.
