# 8. Copy and content inside the design

Edit the words before styling them: one message, a headline that states it, three to five scannable supporting points, one call to action, and every term, number and name checked against the source.

### 8.1 Message discipline

- **One primary message (MUST):** write it as a single sentence before designing. If you cannot, the brief is not ready.
- **One primary call to action (MUST when action is wanted):** secondary actions are visibly weaker.
- Classify every piece of copy as must-see, useful or optional. Optional copy goes to the caption, the next slide, or the bin.
- Word budget for a single social graphic: headline 3–10 words; up to 3–5 short supporting lines of 4–12 words each; footnote one line. More than about 40–50 words on one 1:1 graphic usually means it should be a carousel.

### 8.2 Headlines

- State the benefit, the finding or the tension (“Your land fees may have tripled”), not the category (“Land fee update”).
- Specific beats vague: numbers, names, places and dates.
- Front-load the key word; keep under about 60 characters for social.
- Questions work only when the viewer wants the answer.
- **MUST NOT** promise what the content does not deliver (clickbait erodes trust and may breach advertising codes).

### 8.3 Scannable structure

- Use parallel structure for lists (each item starts the same way grammatically, but not with the same word).
- Lead each bullet with its most informative word.
- Numbers as numerals (“5 things”), not words, in display copy.
- Plain language: short words, active voice, no jargon unless the audience uses it.

### 8.4 Terminology and local accuracy

- **Use the audience’s own professional and local terms.** Example: in Nigerian practice it is a **tenancy agreement**, not a lease, for most residential lets; “prevailing market rent of adjoining properties” is the valuer’s term, not “market rents nearby”.
- Use correct titles and post-nominals (ESV, MNIVS, RSV) exactly as the person uses them.
- Follow the client’s spelling standard (UK English for Crea8Hub and most Nigerian professional work).
- Currency: ₦ symbol before the amount, no space (₦40 m or ₦40m, pick one and keep it); “m” for million, “bn” for billion.
- Dates: 9 November 2026 or 9 Nov 2026; never ambiguous 09/11/26 in public copy.

### 8.5 Calls to action

- Verb first, specific and low-friction: “Send me a message”, “Book a valuation”, “Swipe for details”.
- Place where the reading path ends (bottom-right or bottom-centre for Z-pattern; last slide for carousels).
- Make it visually distinct (accent colour, button shape, or its own line) but not louder than the headline.

### 8.6 Proofreading protocol (MUST, every deliverable)

1. Read every word on the final export, not the draft, at 100% zoom.
2. Check names, titles, phone numbers, emails, URLs, handles, prices, dates, days of the week against the source.
3. Check numbers add up and match the caption and the post text.
4. Check spelling standard, capitalisation system, punctuation (curly quotes, en dashes).
5. Check every claim is supported and every statistic has a source (section 16).
6. Have a second person or a separate AI pass read it cold; the maker’s eye skips errors.
