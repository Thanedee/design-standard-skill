# 18. Design process: brief to approval

Good design is a process: a written brief, research, several concepts, structured critique against the brief, controlled revisions, and measurement after launch. Skipping the brief is the most common cause of wasted rounds.

### 18.1 The Double Diamond

The UK Design Council’s Double Diamond (2004) splits work into two rounds of opening up and narrowing down: **Discover** (understand the real problem), **Define** (frame the challenge), **Develop** (generate and co-create options), **Deliver** (test small, drop what fails, refine what works); the process loops back when findings demand it ([Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/)). **\[Practice/Standard\]**

### 18.2 The creative brief (MUST before design starts)

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Objective (one sentence) | Get Lagos landlords to see rent reviews need evidence, and message ESV Okunade |
| Audience | Landlords and tenants in Lagos, 30–60, on LinkedIn |
| Single message | A fair rent review starts with evidence |
| Deliverables and formats | 1 LinkedIn image, 1080 × 1350 px; alt text; post copy |
| Mandatory content | Exact text, logos supplied, legal lines |
| Brand rules | Palette, fonts, tone, spelling standard, hashtag limits |
| Constraints | No faces; no logos; ARCON approval if it is an advert |
| References | Approved past posts, competitors, mood board |
| Deadline and approver | Date, named approver |
| Success measure | Saves, comments, profile visits, enquiries |

### 18.3 Concepts and iteration

- Produce 2–3 genuinely different concepts at low fidelity before polishing one.
- Present each against the brief: how it delivers the message, not why you like it.
- Limit revision rounds in the contract (typically two or three); bundle feedback per round.
- One change at a time when working with AI (section 17.3).

### 18.4 Critique

Nielsen Norman Group’s critique structure: the presenter restates objectives and asks for specific feedback; critiquers respond against those goals; a facilitator keeps scope and time; feedback is “conversation rather than command”, framed as questions tied to goals rather than personal taste ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/)). **\[Practice\]**

Useful feedback format: **observation** (“the headline and the photo compete”) → **impact** (“the eye doesn’t know where to start”) → **suggestion or question** (“could the photo be darker so the headline leads?”).

### 18.5 Versioning and file hygiene

- Name files: client\_project\_asset\_size\_version\_date (e.g. medinat\_rentreview\_post\_1080x1350\_v03\_2026-11-08.jpg).
- Keep approved masters separate from drafts; never overwrite an approved file.
- Record what changed in each version and who approved it.
- Delete superseded drafts and temporary files after delivery when the client asks for clean storage.

### 18.6 Testing and measurement

- **Before launch:** five-second test, thumbnail test, phone test, colleague cold read.
- **After launch:** compare like with like (same day and time, same format) on signal metrics: saves, shares, comments, profile visits, click-through, enquiries; not just likes.
- **A/B tests:** change one variable (headline, image, layout) at a time; wait for enough impressions before calling a winner.
- Feed results back into templates and this guide.
