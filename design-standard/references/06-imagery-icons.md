# 6. Imagery, photography, illustration and icons

Every image must earn its place by proving, showing or making the viewer feel the message; it must be sharp, honestly represented, cropped with intent, and consistent with the other images in the set.

### 6.1 Choosing images

- **Relevance over beauty (MUST):** the image shows the subject, audience or result the copy is about. A lovely but generic image weakens trust.
- **Specific over generic:** real places, real products, real work and real (consenting) people outperform obvious stock. For a Lagos valuer, Lekki, Ikoyi or Abuja streetscapes beat generic Western suburbs.
- **One subject:** a single clear subject with a simple background. Busy images compete with text.
- **Faces attract the eye first.** Use them when the person is the message. A face looking towards the headline guides attention to it (gaze cueing); a face looking out of the frame leads attention away. **\[Research: gaze-cueing literature; Practice\]**
- **Emotion and action:** people doing the activity convey more than posed portraits.
- **Representation:** show the real audience in age, skin tone, body type, ability and setting. For Nigerian audiences, Nigerian people, dress, architecture and light. Avoid stereotypes and tokenism.

### 6.2 Quality and resolution

| Use | Minimum resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Social post (1080 px wide) | Source at least 1080 px on the shortest needed side; 2× (2160 px) preferred |
| Website hero | 2400–3000 px wide, compressed to under 300–500 KB |
| Print | 300 ppi at final printed size (150–200 ppi acceptable for large posters viewed from afar) |
| Logo | Vector (SVG, EPS, PDF, AI) always |

- **MUST NOT** upscale low-resolution images and pass them off as sharp; AI upscalers invent detail and must never be used on products, documents, faces of real people or property features.
- Check for compression artefacts, banding in skies and gradients, noise and soft focus at 100% zoom.

### 6.3 Image treatment and series consistency

- In a set, keep consistent: lighting direction and time of day, colour grade, crop tightness, background style and camera angle.
- Use one grading approach (warm, cool, high-contrast or natural) per campaign.
- Duotones and colour washes in brand colours unify mixed photography.
- **Overlays:** a gradient or tint should lift text legibility without destroying the photo. Typical values: 30–70% opacity of a dark brand colour, or a directional gradient from the text side.

### 6.4 Cropping and composition in photos

- Rule of thirds for dynamic crops; centre for symmetry and product shots.
- Leave space in the direction the subject faces or moves.
- Never crop at joints, through the chin or the top of the head at an awkward point, or through a product’s label.
- Straighten horizons and vertical lines (architecture especially: correct converging verticals).

### 6.5 Illustration

- Choose one style (flat, line, isometric, 3D, hand-drawn) and stick to it within a campaign.
- Match line weight, corner radius, palette and level of detail across all illustrations.
- Illustration suits abstract ideas (processes, concepts, data); photography suits trust and proof (real places, real people, real products).

### 6.6 Icons

- **One icon family (MUST):** same grid (commonly 24 × 24 px), stroke weight (e.g. 1.5 or 2 px), corner style, fill style (outline or solid, not both) and level of detail.
- Icons support labels; they rarely replace them. Pair icons with text for anything critical.
- Use familiar metaphors (house = property, document = report); test unfamiliar ones.
- Icons need 3:1 contrast against their background (section 5.6).
- Size icons relative to the text they accompany: roughly the cap height to 1.5× the line height.

### 6.7 Photography ethics and authenticity

- Do not retouch body shape or skin in ways that misrepresent; disclose significant retouching in ads where required.
- Property, product and “before and after” images MUST show the real item without hiding defects (section 16).
- Keep model releases for identifiable people and property releases where needed (section 16).
