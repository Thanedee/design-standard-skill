# 16. Legal, ethical and regulatory rules

Only use assets you have the right to use, get consent from identifiable people, make no claim you cannot prove, disclose paid promotion, and in Nigeria get ARCON approval before an advert is published. This section is general guidance, not legal advice; confirm with a lawyer for high-stakes work.

### 16.1 Copyright and licensing

- **Assume everything is protected** unless you created it, licensed it, or it is clearly in the public domain. Nigeria’s Copyright Act 2022 updated protection for the digital age, including digital copies and audio-visual works ([Aluko & Oyebode summary](https://aluko-oyebode.com/insights/key-highlights-of-the-copyright-act-2022/)).
- **Images:** use your own photography, properly licensed stock (keep the licence), commissioned work with a written assignment or licence, or AI output generated under terms that allow commercial use.
- **Fonts are software:** check the licence covers the use (desktop, web, app, broadcast, logo). Google Fonts are open licence; many commercial fonts need separate web or app licences.
- **Music:** commercial licences for brand and ad use (section 12.6).
- **Never** remove watermarks, crop out credits, or trace someone else’s artwork, logo or character.

### 16.2 AI output and ownership

- In the US, the Copyright Office’s January 2025 report concluded that prompts alone do not make a person the author: purely AI-generated output is not copyrightable, but human-authored elements and creative human selection, arrangement and modification can be ([Jones Day, Feb 2025](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/copyrightability-of-ai-outputs-us-copyright-office-analyzes-human-authorship-requirement)). **\[Law/US\]**
- Consequence: add meaningful human design work (layout, typography, edits) to AI imagery for anything that must be owned, such as a logo. **Never** use raw AI output as a trademark without legal advice.
- Do not prompt AI tools to imitate a living artist’s distinctive style, a known character, or a brand’s protected design.

### 16.3 People, likeness and privacy

- Get a signed model release from identifiable people in commercial work; property releases for private interiors and distinctive buildings where required.
- Nigeria’s Data Protection Commission warned in March 2026 that filming or photographing people and sharing it without consent may breach privacy rights under the Constitution and the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, and that creators can face prosecution ([Arise News, 14 Mar 2026](https://www.arise.tv/ndpc-warns-content-creators-against-filming-nigerians-without-consent/)). **\[Law/Nigeria\]**
- **Never** create AI images, voices or deepfakes of real, identifiable people without written consent; never alter a real person’s face in a headshot beyond normal retouching without their approval.

### 16.4 Advertising law and codes

- **ARCON (Nigeria):** adverts aimed at Nigerian consumers, including digital and influencer adverts, must be vetted and approved by ARCON’s Standards Panel before exposure; publishing without approval is an offence, and brands and influencers can be jointly liable ([Mondaq, May 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/social-media/1783774/regulatory-and-compliance-regulations-in-influencer-marketing-and-advertising)). Build at least a week for approval into timelines. **\[Law/Nigeria\]**
- **Disclosure:** paid partnerships must be labelled; the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act (s.126) prohibits misleading endorsements including undisclosed sponsorship (same source).
- **Truthful claims:** every factual claim, statistic, price, comparison and “before and after” must be true and provable; label estimates and reported figures as such; avoid unqualified superlatives (“best in Lagos”) without evidence.
- **Regulated sectors** (finance, health, property investment, alcohol, gambling, education) carry extra rules; for example, investment and returns claims need regulatory review.

### 16.5 Trademarks and third-party brands

- Do not use other companies’ logos or brand names in ways that imply endorsement or partnership that does not exist.
- Platform names and logos (LinkedIn, Instagram) follow the platform’s brand rules.
- National and institutional emblems: see section 15.3.

### 16.6 Ethics beyond the law

- No deceptive patterns (section 14.6) and no false urgency or scarcity.
- No body-shaming, colourism (skin-lightening edits), or stereotyping.
- Respect for the dead, the sick, and victims in news-related content; no exploitation of tragedy for promotion.
- When unsure whether something is acceptable, ask: would the audience feel misled or disrespected if they knew how this was made?
