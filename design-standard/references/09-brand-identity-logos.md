# 9. Brand identity, logos and series consistency

A brand is recognised by a repeatable system (colour, type, layout, imagery, voice), not by the logo alone; protect the logo, codify the system in a guideline, and vary layouts within it so a series feels fresh but unmistakably related.

### 9.1 The identity system

| Component | What to define |
| --- | --- |
| Logo suite | Primary, secondary (stacked/horizontal), icon or mark, monochrome and reversed versions |
| Colour | Primary, secondary, accent, neutrals, with HEX, RGB, CMYK, Pantone, OKLCH (section 5.7) |
| Typography | Display and text families, weights, type scale, case rules |
| Layout | Grid, margins, recurring templates, label and button styles |
| Imagery | Photography style, grading, illustration style, icon family |
| Graphic devices | Shapes, patterns, dividers, frames that recur |
| Voice | Tone, spelling standard, words to use and avoid, hashtag limits |
| Motion | Easing, durations, logo animation, transitions (section 12) |

### 9.2 Logo design principles

Henderson and Cote tested 195 logos and found that **natural** (recognisable, organic) and **harmonious** (balanced, symmetrical) designs with **moderate elaborateness** drive correct recognition and positive feeling; very simple and very complex marks both scored lower on affect ([Journal of Marketing 62(2), 1998](https://newion.uwinnipeg.ca/~ssingh5/Lm/logo-guide.pdf)). **\[Research\]**

Practice rules:

- **Simple enough to work at 16 px** (favicon, profile picture) and on a billboard.
- **Works in one colour** (black, white, single brand colour) before colour is added.
- **Distinctive** in its category; avoid clichés (house roofs for every estate firm, generic swooshes).
- **Timeless over trendy**; avoid effects (gradients, shadows) in the master mark.
- **Built on a construction grid** with consistent stroke weights and optical corrections.
- **Scales and reverses cleanly** (test on dark, light and photographic backgrounds).
- **Legally clear:** search for similar registered marks before adoption; register the trademark.

### 9.3 Logo usage rules (MUST)

- Clear space on all sides, usually defined by a part of the logo (e.g. the height of the letter x).
- Minimum size defined for screen (px) and print (mm).
- Never stretch, rotate, recolour outside approved versions, add effects, outline, or place on busy backgrounds without a container.
- One logo per composition is usually enough; repeating it weakens it.
- Respect partner and client logos: never redraw, recolour or crop them; use only supplied files.

### 9.4 Series consistency with variety

- **Fixed across a series:** palette, typefaces, type scale, label style, margins, icon family, image grading, logo placement.
- **Variable across a series:** layout template (split, full-bleed photo, typographic, comparison, carousel), dominant colour field, image subject, crop.
- Practical method: design 3–5 layout templates from the same system and rotate them so consecutive posts never share the same layout.
- **Carousels and multi-page pieces MUST share one layout** across all slides; variety comes between posts, not within one.
- **Cover test (MUST):** hide the logo; the piece should still be recognisably the brand.

### 9.5 About brand-consistency statistics

Claims such as “consistent branding increases revenue by 33%” come from a vendor survey of about 200 organisations by Lucidpress, a brand-template software company, with no published method for measuring revenue ([PR Newswire, 2019](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/study-finds-companies-with-consistent-branding-can-see-up-to-33-increase-in-revenue-300967219.html)). Treat as marketing, not evidence; the stronger argument for consistency is the fluency and mere-exposure research in section 1.2.
