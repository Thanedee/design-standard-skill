# The Complete Design Guide

This guide is the governing standard for any visual that leaves this studio, whether a person or an AI model (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) makes it. Clarity comes first, hierarchy second, consistency third; aesthetics serve the message and never hide it.

## 0. How to use this guide

**Scope.** Static graphics, carousels, motion and video, print, presentations, brand identity, web and app screens, and AI-generated imagery. Sections 1–2 explain why design works; sections 3–16 set the rules; section 17 is the guardrail system for AI output; sections 18–20 cover process, delivery and quality control.

**How rules are graded.**

| Grade | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MUST | A breach fails the design, whatever its other scores | Text contrast below 4.5:1 for body copy |
| SHOULD | Default practice; break it only for a stated reason | One or two type families per piece |
| MAY | A good option, used by judgement | A third display face for a campaign accent |

**Evidence levels.** Each rule rests on one of three kinds of support, marked where it matters:

- **\[Research\]** peer-reviewed findings in perception, cognition, readability or colour science.
- **\[Standard\]** formal standards: WCAG 2.2, ISO 12647 (print), platform specifications, law and advertising codes.
- **\[Practice\]** settled professional craft (Swiss grid tradition, typographic convention) that has no single study behind it but near-universal agreement among practitioners.

**For AI models.** When you are asked to create or edit a visual, treat every MUST in this guide as a hard constraint, state the brief back before generating, spell every word exactly as supplied, invent no facts, figures, logos or emblems, and run the section 17 verification protocol on your own output before presenting it.

**For human designers.** Read sections 1–2 once, keep sections 3–16 as reference, and run section 20 on every deliverable. Where the brand guideline conflicts with this guide, the brand guideline wins on identity (colour, type, logo) and this guide wins on legibility, accuracy and accessibility.

**The order of judgement.** When rules pull against each other, resolve them in this order: (1) truth and legality, (2) legibility and accessibility, (3) hierarchy and message, (4) brand consistency, (5) aesthetic preference.
